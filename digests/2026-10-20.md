# AI News Digest - 2026-10-20

## Top stories

### OpenAI is reportedly rushing a raise at over $1.4T to close before Anthropic's IPO
Two people told Reuters that OpenAI wants investor commitments in place before Anthropic's expected listing in mid-November. The round could value it at more than $1.4 trillion. Other outlets put the size at about $30B, with SoftBank and Abu Dhabi's MGX in talks to join. OpenAI is telling investors it has $50B in annualised revenue and expects $70B by year-end. Anthropic told investors its quarterly revenue rose to $16.8B in Q3 from $11.6B in Q2, though slower month-to-month growth has worried some of them. **Why it matters:** The two biggest labs are now competing for the same investors, and Anthropic's listing will put the first public price on a frontier lab.
Sources: [The Star (Reuters)](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/20/exclusive-openai-seeks-to-accelerate-fundraising-ahead-of-anthropic-ipo-sources-say), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-fundraising-ahead-anthropic-ipo/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/19/anthropic-ipo-ai-trade.html)

### Researcher says one hacker used AI agents to break into 100+ companies' sites
Snorre Fagerland of the security firm NorForge watched a Chinese-speaking hacker leave almost all the work to AI agents. The agents ran on Cairn, an open-source AI problem-solving platform. Starting on Sept 25 they went after more than 1,200 sites, mostly online shops in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan. Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins were among the victims. Fagerland estimates the campaign used about $5,000 of tokens, or roughly $50 per successful break-in. These are estimates, not billing records. **Why it matters:** This is a detailed case of agents carrying out break-ins from start to finish, and it shows one person can now run attacks at a scale that used to need a team.
Sources: [Reuters via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/exclusive-one-hacker-used-ai-100625247.html), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-agents-hacker-cairn-100-sites-deere-norforge), [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/chinese-hacker-use-ai-agent-to-hack/)

### US DHS orders DeepSeek removed from all federal civilian systems
Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 tells civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products, and any third-party services built on its models. They are also barred from buying them in future. DHS cited the risk of sensitive data going to the Chinese government, possible malware and supply-chain risk. Until now, agencies had decided for themselves whether to block such models. **Why it matters:** The order reaches tools that are merely built on DeepSeek, so software vendors that sell to the US government now have to check what models their products use.
Sources: [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/dhs-bans-deepseek-federal-agencies/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2290535/homeland-security-bans-deepseek-use-in-federal-civilian-agencies/), [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/us-agencies-remove-deepseek-products-government-systems-1826567)

### Germany signs its industrial-AI deal with Nvidia
*Update to yesterday's item: the deal has now been signed.* At the Invest in Germany Summit on Monday, the economy ministry signed a memorandum of understanding with Nvidia. The ministry gave no other details, so the reported state funding of hundreds of millions of euros is still unconfirmed. Nvidia says Chancellor Merz and two ministries have agreed a joint plan on "physical AI" for robots, machines and cars. It also named four German startups it plans to back as part of its nearly $10B commitment in Europe: Agile Robots, Avelios Medical, Mirelo and Reflex Aerospace. **Why it matters:** Europe's largest industrial economy is basing its industrial-AI plan on Nvidia's technology, at the same moment as the debate over AI sovereignty.
Sources: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/germany-nvidia-ai-partnership-berlin-summit), [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/)

## Policy & regulation
- **The EU publishes the first harmonised standard for the AI Act** - On Oct 19 the Commission published in the Official Journal the reference for the first harmonised standard supporting the Act. It covers quality management systems (prEN 18286). This gives companies their first official route to showing they comply. ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-act-standardisation))
- **Canada's biggest publishers sue Anthropic in Ontario** - Postmedia, Torstar, The Globe and Mail and The Canadian Press say Anthropic copied their content to train Claude, broke their sites' terms of service and got around anti-copying measures. The claim was filed Oct 6 and reported Oct 19. None of it has been proven in court. ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/anthropic-postmedia-torstar-globe-canadian-press-copyright-lawsuit-9.7387205), [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-canadian-news-publishers-sue-anthropic-for-copyright-infringement/))
